# Usurpia and Arcadia: a systems inquiry *An introductory inquiry from ATOTSM — a conceptual essay, not a completed empirical study.* ## The question Many social problems are discussed as though they were unrelated: financial pressure, constrained choices, institutional incentives, ecological strain, unequal access to resources, and the difficulty of building a life around what matters. Could some of these conditions share underlying dynamics? What assumptions, incentives, and structures shape the possibilities available to people? What changes when we investigate roots as well as symptoms? This is the question motivating ATOTSM. It is an invitation to investigate—not a demand to accept a predetermined conclusion. ## Two conditions, one world **Usurpia** names a condition in which the dynamics of usury and their consequences remain insufficiently recognised. In this framework, the inquiry is wider than the headline interest rate: it asks how monetary arrangements interact with institutions, relationships, choices, and the distribution of power. **Arcadia** names a condition of increasing awareness, human possibility, cooperation, and the creation of alternatives. They are not geographical places, separate societies, or two kinds of people. They can coexist in the same world, community, relationship, and person. The metaphorical **Exodus** is a movement from unawareness toward awareness and possibility here and now—not a physical departure from society. These terms are conceptual lenses. Their usefulness depends on whether they help people notice, investigate, and explain something more clearly. ## Why investigate systems rather than isolated symptoms? A systems inquiry asks how elements interact: - What incentives reward or discourage particular choices? - Which costs are visible, and which are displaced onto others? - How do institutions and habits reinforce one another? - Which historical arrangements enabled real capabilities—and what dependencies accompanied them? - What alternatives become imaginable when we distinguish a system's useful functions from its harmful or limiting dynamics? The framework proposes that usury should be investigated as a systemic phenomenon, not reduced to the ordinary question of whether a particular interest rate is high or low. That proposal needs careful work. A systems lens can reveal connections, but it can also over-connect unrelated events. Each specific causal claim therefore needs its own definitions, sources, method, counterarguments, and limits. ## The rocket-booster metaphor A rocket booster can provide acceleration that makes a journey possible. But acceleration is not the same as a destination, and a useful temporary mechanism does not automatically justify permanent dependence. ATOTSM uses this metaphor to ask a historical and practical question: **what capabilities did debt-based monetary arrangements help mobilise, what costs and dependencies developed alongside them, and how might societies retain useful capabilities while creating more balanced alternatives?** The metaphor is a prompt for investigation—not, by itself, proof of a historical causal account. ## Two mutually enriching inquiries ### Usurology Usurology is proposed as an interdisciplinary investigation of usury as a systemic phenomenon: its structures, dynamics, consequences, and persistent unawareness. It asks how monetary arrangements interact with personal, relational, societal, environmental, moral, and institutional dimensions. Its aim is to investigate underlying dynamics rather than endlessly treating symptoms separately. ### Ikigaiology / Arcadialogy Ikigaiology, within this wider vision, explores human development, purpose, talents, interests, knowledge, capabilities, and flourishing. Arcadialogy names the broader open philosophy of cultivating possibility and creating alternatives. This is not a compulsory curriculum or a demand to monetise every talent. People can develop evolving personal libraries according to their own curiosity and direction. The inquiries are mutually enriching, not consecutive stages: understanding changes what we choose to create, while practical experience changes how we understand the world. ## From individual possibility to collective flourishing The proposed pathway is emergent: **Individuals → Relationships → Communities → Society** - Individuals discover and cultivate capabilities. - Relationships connect different contributions. - Communities organise cooperation around actual needs and shared possibilities. - Wider change may emerge from flourishing relationships and communities. This is not a centrally imposed blueprint. It is a direction to explore through real people, places, constraints, and experiments. Complementary currencies are one possible part of this exploration. Different currencies may help recognise and connect different forms of contribution, time, resources, skills, and capabilities. They belong within a wider plural monetary ecology—not as a single universal solution and not as a replacement monopoly. Other possibilities include peer-to-peer cooperation, open source, digital commons, cooperative structures, permaculture, food forests, and self-organising initiatives. Each requires its own design and evaluation. ## How to examine the framework This page introduces a conceptual framework. It does **not** establish that every social problem is caused by usury, that every proposed alternative will work, or that the framework has been empirically validated. A disciplined inquiry can proceed by: 1. **Define the claim.** What specific dynamic is being proposed? 2. **Trace the mechanism.** Through what steps could it produce the stated consequence? 3. **Identify evidence.** What observations, records, data, or cases support or challenge it? 4. **Compare explanations.** What other mechanisms could account for the same outcome? 5. **State boundaries.** Where does the claim apply, and where might it not? 6. **Make the proposal inspectable.** What design, model, or experiment could help test it? 7. **Revise openly.** What would change the conclusion? This is the bridge between the Sovereign Library's inquiry and the Sovereign Laboratory's practical exploration. ## Where to go next - **Sovereign Library** — explore the developing maps, narratives, instruments, proposals, and media. - **Sovereign Laboratory** — explore practical designs, play, currency prototypes, digital commons, and ecological or community experiments. - **Method** — see ATOTSM's stated evidence and inquiry posture. - **Explore** — choose a way into the wider body of work. ## Status and revision **Status:** Published as a conceptual introduction in this proposed milestone; not an empirical study or validated causal model. **Source basis:** ATOTSM's V2 narrative blueprint and the current public site's stated conceptual framing. **Open work:** Link specific claims to inspectable sources and artifacts; publish worked examples; document counterarguments and revisions as the underlying work becomes available. --- *The invitation is to investigate together: understand inherited conditions, discover neglected possibilities, and explore what we can create better together.*
