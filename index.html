USUROLOGY × IKIGAIOLOGY   ·   UNDERSTANDING × FLOURISHING
The central proposition

Better lives require more than better intentions.

Human flourishing is shaped by the systems we inherit, the rules we live under, the resources we can access, the relationships we build and the possibilities we are able to imagine.

Understanding those conditions and cultivating meaningful lives are not separate projects. They belong in the same conversation.

We need to understand what shapes our lives — and become more capable of shaping the conditions in which life can flourish.
Two lenses, one inquiry

See the conditions.
See the possibilities.

Usurology and Ikigaiology are proposed, evolving fields of inquiry. They offer different but complementary lenses. Neither needs to claim the whole picture.

U

Usurology

The study of systems and conditions

An inquiry into how the structures around us shape what people and communities can do: who has agency, how resources move, which rules govern participation and where power accumulates.

  • Institutions, law and governance
  • Money, ownership and economic systems
  • Technology, infrastructure and access
  • Incentives, power and distributions of agency
I

Ikigaiology

The study of meaningful flourishing

An inquiry into what makes life worth living and what helps people develop their capacities: purpose, belonging, vitality, contribution, autonomy and meaningful connection.

  • Meaning, purpose and direction
  • Health, vitality and human needs
  • Relationships, belonging and community
  • Mastery, contribution and personal agency
U × I
The meeting point

What kind of systems support meaningful lives — and what kind of lives, in turn, help create healthy systems? The relationship is reciprocal.

From inquiry to practice

A framework should help us do something.

The aim is not to produce another closed ideology. It is to make ideas examinable, useful and open to revision through experience.

01

Observe

Look closely at lived experience, evidence, needs and existing conditions.

02

Understand

Trace relationships, incentives, constraints, causes and consequences.

03

Imagine

Explore alternatives, principles, possibilities and better arrangements.

04

Experiment

Try things at an appropriate scale, learn from results and revise.

A whole-of-life perspective

Flourishing is not one-dimensional.

A society can be wealthy yet lonely, technologically advanced yet disempowering, productive yet ecologically destructive. A useful framework must look across the dimensions of life and how they interact.

01

Material foundations

Food, shelter, security, resources and the means to live.

02

Health & vitality

Physical and mental wellbeing, energy, care and resilience.

03

Relationships

Love, friendship, trust, belonging and mutual support.

04

Agency & freedom

Autonomy, meaningful choice, voice and influence over one's life.

05

Meaning & mastery

Purpose, learning, creativity, contribution and the development of potential.

06

Community & ecology

Shared life, culture, place, biodiversity and a thriving living world.

Intellectual honesty

Open to scrutiny. Built to evolve.

A new vocabulary is not proof that a new discipline is valid. These concepts should earn their usefulness through clarity, evidence, criticism and practice. Different kinds of claims deserve to be identified rather than blended together.

Evidence

Observations, data and sources that others can inspect and question.

Interpretation

An explanation of what evidence may mean, with its assumptions visible.

Hypothesis

A proposition that can be investigated and potentially disproved.

Proposal

A suggested principle, design or intervention that invites evaluation.

Counterargument

The strongest objections, alternatives and inconvenient evidence.

Revision

The willingness to change a position when better reasons emerge.
