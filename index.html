A framework for understanding and transforming the conditions of existence

Understand the conditions.
Create better ones.

Our lives are shaped by more than individual choices. They are shaped by institutions, money, culture, ecology, relationships, technologies and the possibilities available to us. ATOTSM brings two complementary inquiries together: understanding the systems that shape life, and understanding what allows life to flourish.

The central proposition

A better life needs a better environment for life.

Personal wellbeing matters. So do the conditions within which people try to live well. A person may develop resilience, skills and purpose, yet still face institutions, incentives or material constraints that narrow their choices. Conversely, changing a system without asking what people actually need can create a different kind of failure.

The work therefore moves in both directions: from structures to lived experience, and from human needs back to the design of structures.

The question is not only “How can a person adapt to the world?” It is also “What kind of world are we creating — and what could it become?”
Two complementary lenses

Diagnose the architecture.
Design for flourishing.

These are proposed, evolving fields of inquiry — not claims of established academic consensus. Their value has to be demonstrated through clear concepts, open criticism and useful work.

I

Usurology

Investigate the systems and conditions shaping human agency: money, debt, ownership, law, governance, institutions, technology, energy and ecology.

II

Ikigaiology

Investigate meaningful flourishing: material security, health, belonging, autonomy, purpose, mastery, contribution and connection with the living world.

U × I
Integration is the point.

Systems are evaluated by the lives they enable; ideas of flourishing are tested against the systems and resources needed to make them possible.

From understanding to creation

A map that leads somewhere.

ATOTSM is not meant to end as a theory about what is wrong. It should help people examine, design, test and improve real alternatives.

01

Understand

Map systems, incentives, constraints, power and lived effects.

02

Reimagine

Define what a flourishing life and community would require.

03

Design

Combine institutions, tools, currencies and relationships into alternatives.

04

Test

Run experiments, measure outcomes, expose failures and revise.

A whole-of-life lens

Flourishing has many dimensions.

No single metric can tell us whether a person or community is flourishing. These domains are a working map, not a rigid hierarchy or a universal formula.

01

Material foundations

Food, shelter, energy, income, tools and security.

02

Health & vitality

Physical and mental health, care, time and resilience.

03

Belonging & relationships

Trust, friendship, family, mutual aid and community.

04

Agency & sovereignty

Real choice, voice, self-direction and shared power.

05

Meaning & mastery

Purpose, learning, creativity, skill and contribution.

06

Ecology & continuity

Place, biodiversity, stewardship and future generations.

The practical laboratory

What could better conditions look like?

One important test is the local community: can people meet more of their needs through a diverse, cooperative ecosystem of exchange, shared assets and democratic decisions?

A

Plural currencies

Explore mutual credit, time exchange, local currencies, commons credits and ordinary money as tools with different functions.

B

Community capacity

Map skills, spaces, tools, food, care, knowledge and underused resources that can be connected.

C

Shared governance

Design transparent rules, consent, accountability, dispute resolution and safeguards against capture.

Intellectual standards

Open to scrutiny.
Built to evolve.

A compelling idea is not automatically a true one. We distinguish observations from interpretations, hypotheses from findings, and proposals from proven solutions. Counterarguments and failure conditions belong in the framework itself.

01

Evidence

Observations, data and sources that others can inspect.

02

Interpretation

Explanations with assumptions made visible.

03

Hypothesis

Testable propositions that could be weakened or rejected.
