The aim is not to become the public face of every initiative or to execute every alternative. It is to deepen awareness, connect ideas and inspire the thinkers, educators and community builders who will carry understanding into practice.

The question beneath the questions

What if seemingly separate problems share underlying dynamics?

We often approach the world one problem at a time: a difficulty here, a crisis there, a new policy or a temporary fix. But what shapes the conditions in which these problems arise? Which assumptions, incentives and structures influence what we can see, choose and change?

ATOTSM grew from a deeper question: what if many things called “roots” were themselves consequences? The investigation had to go further—beneath accepted explanations, toward the conditions and dynamics producing them—and connect that understanding with the possibility of creating something better.

The central proposition

A better life needs a better environment for life.

Personal wellbeing matters. So do the conditions within which people try to live well. A person may develop resilience, skills and purpose, yet still face institutions, incentives or material constraints that narrow their choices. Conversely, changing a system without asking what people actually need can create a different kind of failure.

The work therefore moves in both directions: from structures to lived experience, and from human needs back to the design of structures.

The aspiration is alignment: what genuinely nourishes a person should, as far as possible, also nourish relationships, communities, wider society, the living world and future generations. This is not automatic. Self-interest can become exploitation; communities can favour insiders; good intentions can shift costs elsewhere. Awareness, reciprocity, justice and ecological responsibility help bring these dimensions into coherence.

The question is not only “How can a person adapt to the world?” It is also “What kind of world are we creating — and what could it become?”
Human possibility

A life is larger than a job title.

People are not single-purpose economic units. Each person carries a changing constellation of interests, talents, questions, knowledge and capabilities. Arcadialogy / Ikigaiology explores how these can grow, connect and contribute — without turning every passion into a profession.

01

Explore

Follow curiosity across the practical, physical, creative, intellectual, social, civic and contemplative dimensions of life.

02

Develop

Build understanding and capability through experience, study, practice, reflection, making, failure and discovery.

03

Connect & contribute

Bring abilities into relationships, shared projects and forms of contribution that remain meaningful to the people involved.

That exploration might involve cultivating food or ecosystems; making, repairing or building; caring, teaching or organising; inventing, designing or coding; composing, performing or telling stories; investigating science, history or philosophy; playing sport; exploring cultures; or developing forms of craft, service and civic participation. The list is deliberately open.

Concentric flourishing: personal wellbeing → healthy relationships → cooperative communities → just institutions and society → ecological care and future generations. These dimensions can reinforce one another, but their alignment has to be designed and continually tested.

A small, concrete example

What is already inside the price?

Margrit Kennedy’s Interest and Inflation Free Money argues that costs associated with capital and interest can reach people through prices and fees, even when they have not borrowed personally. Her book illustrates this with examples for garbage collection, drinking water and social housing.

In the passage accompanying Figure 3, Kennedy describes the figures as the capital share of costs — 12%, 38% and 77% respectively. Capital costs are not automatically identical to interest paid. The exact financing component, its pass-through through suppliers and its eventual incidence on households need to be traced rather than assumed.

ATOTSM extends the question: what financing costs, fees and margins accumulate at each stage of a supply chain, who ultimately bears them, and how do they affect purchasing power and public budgets? These costs can operate like a hidden burden on people’s purchasing power. “Hidden tax” describes that effect metaphorically; it does not mean every cost is a tax under law.

Behind the scenes · Discreet assistance

A quiet influence. Independent people.

ATOTSM is not built around a public personality, a guru or a central authority. Its purpose is to develop understanding that others can carry into their own work, communities and initiatives.

The central inquiry begins with usury awareness: recognising how monetary arrangements, debt, interest, fees and wider incentives may shape personal, relational, communal, societal and ecological conditions. From there, people can investigate complementary currencies and other forms of cooperation—not as fashionable technical fixes, but as alternatives whose purpose and consequences deserve to be understood.

The people who build, teach and operate those alternatives should be able to make them their own. ATOTSM's contribution is more meta: philosophical inquiry, connecting patterns, challenging assumptions, offering perspective and helping the people who inspire and organise tomorrow to deepen their own understanding.

Some of that work may happen quietly, through private conversations, confidential research, thoughtful introductions or behind-the-scenes guidance. The person or community doing the visible work remains the protagonist. Assistance is shaped around the question and agreed scope; it is not a promise to control outcomes or a substitute for specialist professional advice.

Complementary-currency design can be part of a collaboration, but never detached from the awareness and systemic inquiry that give it meaning.

A few words used here

A small guide to the language.

These terms are invitations into a shared vocabulary, not tests of belonging.

Usurpia

A condition in which dynamics of usury and their consequences remain insufficiently recognised.

Arcadia

A condition of increasing awareness, flourishing, cooperation and practical possibility — not a distant perfect society.

Modern-day Exodus

An awakening and constructive movement away from unexamined patterns and towards awareness and alternatives within the society we already inhabit. It is not a call for physical migration or withdrawal from the world.

Complementary currency

A currency or exchange system that serves alongside other arrangements. Any currency — including a national or state-issued one — can play a complementary role within a plural monetary ecology.

Emergineering

A developing term for consciously shaping conditions in which useful possibilities can emerge, while learning from feedback and unintended effects.

An open inquiry

Curiosity without compulsory agreement.

A compelling idea is not automatically a true one. We distinguish observations from interpretations, hypotheses from findings, and proposals from proven solutions. Counterarguments and failure conditions belong in the framework itself.

You do not have to agree with a conclusion to investigate it.
