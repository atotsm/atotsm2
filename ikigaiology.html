Lens two · Human flourishing

What makes a life
worth living?

Ikigaiology is a proposed inquiry into meaningful flourishing: what people need, what helps them develop their capacities, how they find direction and belonging, and how individual lives connect to the wellbeing of others and the living world.

Working definition

Flourishing is more than feeling good or being productive.

It includes the ability to meet needs, experience safety and connection, exercise agency, develop capabilities, find meaning and contribute. It also depends on social and ecological conditions beyond any individual's control.

Purpose should help people live more fully. It should never become another instrument for demanding more performance from them.
A whole-of-life map

Six interacting dimensions.

This is a working map for reflection and design, not a rigid ladder. Needs overlap; their priority changes with circumstance, culture, disability, age, crisis and personal preference.

01 · Foundations

Material security

Food, water, shelter, warmth, income, tools and access.

02 · Vitality

Health & care

Physical and mental wellbeing, rest, safety and support.

03 · Connection

Belonging

Friendship, family, trust, mutual aid and community.

04 · Agency

Freedom & voice

Autonomy, consent, meaningful choice and influence.

05 · Development

Meaning & mastery

Purpose, learning, creativity, competence and contribution.

06 · Continuity

Reciprocity & ecology

Care for place, nature and future generations.

Maslow as a conversation partner, not a cage. A needs hierarchy can help notice foundational conditions, but real lives do not always follow a fixed sequence. People pursue meaning amid insecurity; belonging can create resilience; needs are culturally and individually shaped.
The purpose question

Ikigai is not a productivity hack.

The popular four-circle diagram is one modern interpretation of a richer Japanese term and cultural context. This project uses Ikigaiology as a starting point for inquiry, not as a claim to define Japanese experience or reduce purpose to a formula.

01

Purpose is personal

What feels meaningful cannot simply be assigned by an employer, movement, family or framework.

02

Purpose is relational

Meaning often grows through relationships, care, place, shared work and contribution.

03

Purpose needs conditions

Time, health, resources, freedom and supportive communities can make meaningful activity possible.

04

Purpose can change

Life stages, responsibilities, losses and discoveries can alter what matters.

From individual to collective

A flourishing person needs a flourishing context.

Ikigaiology should not individualise social problems. A person cannot meditate their way into secure housing, manufacture accessible healthcare, or create free time where every hour is already committed to survival.

A

Personal practice

Reflect on needs, values, energy, relationships, capabilities and meaningful contribution.

B

Shared infrastructure

Build access to care, learning, public spaces, tools, food, mobility and participation.

C

Collective agency

Give people real influence over the rules, resources and institutions shaping common life.
